\begin{abstract}
Quantised instruction-tuned language models are usually validated with perplexity and multiple-choice accuracy.
We show that these metrics can miss a complete loss of instruction following.
At 3 bits, GPTQ under the calibration protocol that a major library uses by default lowers IFEval from 0.77 to 0.15 on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and from 0.82 to 0.43 on Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, below round-to-nearest (0.57 and 0.70), while MMLU stays in the ordinary 3-bit range.
The failure is rare (2 of 18 models) and depends on how calibration text is sampled.
We trace it to one weight matrix: the MLP down-projection of the early layer in which the attention sink forms.
Its calibration Hessian is effectively rank-one, dominated by the beginning-of-sequence token, whereas the loss is insensitive to that token's output and sensitive to the prompt-template tokens that attend to it.
The layer-wise objective, which uses only the input side, therefore optimises for the one token that does not matter.
Under a dominant token the GPTQ update has an exact closed form that predicts the matrix, row and direction of the damage and separates it from ordinary rounding noise.
The damaged matrix is harmless inside the full-precision model; collapse requires the rest of a GPTQ-quantised network.
Weighting each calibration token by its output-side sensitivity divided by its input energy restores the missing curvature: it eliminates all observed 3-bit collapses and, on 16 models, never falls more than 4.6 points below round-to-nearest, at an average gain of 0.3 points on non-collapsing models.
The curvature mismatch is present in all 26 models with an attention sink and absent in the two without.
\end{abstract}
