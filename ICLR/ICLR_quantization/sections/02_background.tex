\section{Background}
\label{sec:setup}
Two objects meet in this paper: the objective that layer-wise quantisers minimise, and the attention sink that instruction-tuned models form in their first layers.
We introduce each with the prior work it comes from, and state the assumption that their meeting violates.

\paragraph{The layer-wise objective and its lineage.}
For a linear layer $\mW\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ with calibration inputs $\vx_1,\dots,\vx_T$, post-training quantisation chooses $\hat\mW$ to minimise the layer's reconstruction error,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:layerwise}
\min_{\hat\mW}\;\operatorname{tr}\big[(\hat\mW-\mW)\,\hess\,(\hat\mW-\mW)^\top\big],
\qquad \hess=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\vx_t\vx_t^\top .
\end{equation}
The curvature $\hess$ descends from Optimal Brain Damage and Surgeon \citep{lecun1990obd,hassibi1993obs}, which used the Hessian of the loss; OBC and GPTQ \citep{frantar2022obc,frantar2023gptq} replaced it by the input covariance of each layer, which needs no gradient, and made the method tractable at scale.
GPTQ rounds one column at a time and compensates each rounding error $\delta_j$ on the columns not yet rounded by $\Delta w_k=-\delta_j[\hess^{-1}]_{jk}/[\hess^{-1}]_{jj}$, after adding a damping $\lambda\mI$ with $\lambda=\rho\operatorname{mean}\operatorname{diag}\hess$ and ordering columns by $\operatorname{diag}\hess$ (act-order); round-to-nearest (RTN) skips the compensation.
Later work refined the update by asymmetric targets \citep{li2025gptaq}, a lattice view with error bounds \citep{chen2026babai}, Hessian conditioning \citep{zhang2026heroq,kim2026dashq} or rotations that remove outliers before rounding \citep{chee2023quip,ashkboos2024quarot}; other methods learn rounding by a token-averaged block loss \citep{nagel2020adaround,li2021brecq,shao2024omniquant,cheng2024autoround}, keep sensitive weights at higher precision \citep{dettmers2024spqr,kim2024squeezellm,xiao2025tacq}, or only rescale channels \citep{lin2024awq}.
All of them inherit two assumptions of \cref{eq:layerwise}: each token enters $\hess$ with weight $\|\vx_t\|^2$, and an output error of a given size is equally harmful at every position.
A few recent methods reintroduce the loss into the objective, through an output-channel curvature \citep{lee2026kronq,kim2025guidedquant} or a local token saliency \citep{xue2025vlmq,sung2025rsq}; KronQ observes that a channel-level factor cancels inside the column-wise update.
We return to them in \cref{sec:mechanism}, where the relevant axis turns out to be the token, not the channel.

\paragraph{Attention sinks.}
Decoder-only models route a large share of attention to the first token, and the residual stream at that position grows in the first layers to a norm hundreds of times larger than elsewhere \citep{xiao2024streamingllm,sun2024massive,an2025systematic}; the sink emerges with training \citep{gu2025sink}, acts as a default target that prevents over-mixing \citep{barbero2025firsttoken}, is written by a few weights of one early down-projection \citep{yu2024superweight}, has a nearly constant direction \citep{chen2026spikes}, and, because RMSNorm divides by the norm, passes almost no gradient back to itself \citep{chen2026gradientsinks}.
For a quantiser this means that one calibration token contributes almost all of $\hess$ at the sink-forming layer while contributing almost nothing to the loss, which is where the two assumptions above fail together.

\paragraph{Calibration data and what evaluation misses.}
That GPTQ is sensitive to its calibration set is documented \citep{williams2024calibration}, calibrating instruction-tuned models on chat-formatted text is folklore in the libraries, attributed to the private post-training data \citep{lee2025upq}, and normalising by sample length has been proposed to stabilise $\hess$ \citep{son2026maca}.
Empirical surveys report that GPTQ beats RTN on perplexity at every bit-width \citep{huang2024llama3quant,zhou2026signalnoise} and model the loss from precision as smooth \citep{kumar2025precision}; that likelihood-based metrics miss what compression does has been argued for task accuracy \citep{jaiswal2024kick,lee2025tradeoffs,hu2026damage}, for alignment under KV-cache quantisation \citep{xu2026alignmentcollapse}, for generation quality \citep{lee2026lfq}, and for a distinct 2-bit failure of early-layer computation \citep{zhou2026twofailure}.
The failure studied here is at 3 bits, in one matrix, and has a cause at the level of the objective.
