\section{Background}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{The layer-wise objective.}
For a linear layer $\mW\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ with calibration inputs $\vx_1,\dots,\vx_T$, post-training quantization chooses $\hat\mW$ to minimize
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:layerwise}
\operatorname{tr}\big[(\hat\mW-\mW)\,\hess\,(\hat\mW-\mW)^\top\big],
\qquad \hess=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\vx_t\vx_t^\top .
\end{equation}
Following GPTQ we call $\hess$ the calibration Hessian; it is the Hessian of the reconstruction error, the input covariance, not the Hessian of the loss, which is the distinction \cref{sec:mechanism} turns on: Optimal Brain Surgeon \citep{lecun1990obd,hassibi1993obs} used the loss Hessian, OBC and GPTQ \citep{frantar2022obc,frantar2023gptq} replaced it by the input covariance.
GPTQ rounds one column at a time and compensates each rounding error $\delta_j$ on the columns not yet rounded by $\Delta w_k=-\delta_j[\hess^{-1}]_{jk}/[\hess^{-1}]_{jj}$, after damping $\hess$ by $\lambda\mI$, $\lambda=\rho\operatorname{mean}\operatorname{diag}\hess$, and ordering columns by $\operatorname{diag}\hess$ (act-order); round-to-nearest (RTN) skips the compensation.
Later work refines the update \citep{li2025gptaq,chen2026babai,zhang2026heroq,kim2026dashq}, rotates outliers away first \citep{chee2023quip,ashkboos2024quarot}, learns rounding by a token-averaged block loss \citep{nagel2020adaround,li2021brecq,shao2024omniquant,cheng2024autoround}, keeps sensitive weights at higher precision \citep{dettmers2024spqr,kim2024squeezellm,xiao2025tacq} or rescales channels \citep{lin2024awq}; all inherit the two assumptions of \cref{eq:layerwise}, that each token enters $\hess$ with weight $\|\vx_t\|^2$ and that an output error is equally harmful at every position, and the methods that reintroduce the loss do so through an output-channel curvature \citep{lee2026kronq,kim2025guidedquant} or a token saliency \citep{xue2025vlmq,sung2025rsq}; a saliency read from attention concentration, as in RSQ, ranks the sink token first, the opposite of what \cref{prop:tokenlevel} requires.

\paragraph{Attention sinks and calibration.}
Decoder-only models route much of their attention to the first token, whose residual stream grows in the first layers to a norm hundreds of times larger than elsewhere \citep{xiao2024streamingllm,sun2024massive,an2025systematic}; the sink emerges with training \citep{gu2025sink}, prevents over-mixing \citep{barbero2025firsttoken}, is written by a few weights of one early down-projection \citep{yu2024superweight}, has a nearly constant direction \citep{chen2026spikes} and, because RMSNorm divides by the norm, passes almost no gradient back to itself \citep{chen2026gradientsinks}: for a quantizer, one calibration token contributes almost all of $\hess$ at the sink-forming layer and almost nothing to the loss.
GPTQ's sensitivity to its calibration set \citep{williams2024calibration}, chat-formatted calibration \citep{lee2025upq} and length normalization of $\hess$ \citep{son2026maca} are known; surveys report GPTQ beating RTN on perplexity at every bit-width \citep{huang2024llama3quant,zhou2026signalnoise,kumar2025precision}, while likelihood metrics miss what compression does to task accuracy \citep{jaiswal2024kick,lee2025tradeoffs,hu2026damage}, alignment \citep{xu2026alignmentcollapse}, generation \citep{lee2026lfq} and early-layer computation at 2 bits \citep{zhou2026twofailure}; the failure studied here is at 3 bits, in one matrix, with a cause at the level of the objective.
