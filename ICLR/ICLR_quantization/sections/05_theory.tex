\section{Theory: what the layer-wise objective leaves out}
\label{sec:mechanism}
The lesion has three properties that a mechanism must produce: it arises in the sink-forming layer and nowhere else, it is written along one direction and concentrated in a few rows, and it damages the tokens that overlap the sink pattern while sparing the rest.
This section derives all three from \cref{eq:layerwise}, and derives the repair.
Throughout, $\mW\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ is one layer, $\vx_t$ its input at position $t$, $\vx_0$ the input at the \bos{} position, and $\hess=\sum_t\vx_t\vx_t^\top$.
GPTQ rounds column $j$ to $q_j$ with error vector $\delta_j=\vw_j-q_j\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ over rows and updates every unrounded column $k$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:obs}
\Delta\vw_k=-\,\delta_j\,\frac{[\hess_\lambda^{-1}]_{jk}}{[\hess_\lambda^{-1}]_{jj}},\qquad \hess_\lambda=\hess+\lambda\mI,
\end{equation}
the change that best cancels the effect of $\delta_j$ on the layer output under \cref{eq:layerwise}.

\subsection{Two sides of the curvature}
\label{sec:mechanism:sides}
\begin{lemma}[two-sided curvature]
\label{lem:twosides}
Let $L$ be the loss of the network and $\vy_t=\mW\vx_t$.
To second order in $\Delta\mW$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:twosides}
\Delta L=\tfrac12\sum_{t}(\Delta\mW\vx_t)^\top\mG_t(\Delta\mW\vx_t)+o(\|\Delta\mW\|^2),\qquad \mG_t=\frac{\partial^2L}{\partial\vy_t\,\partial\vy_t^\top},
\end{equation}
so the Hessian of $L$ in $\operatorname{vec}(\mW)$ is $\sum_t\vx_t\vx_t^\top\otimes\mG_t$ \citep{martens2015kfac}, and \cref{eq:layerwise} is the case $\mG_t\equiv\mI$.
\end{lemma}
The substitution keeps the input side, which is free, and drops the output side, which needs a backward pass.
It is harmless only if $\mG_t$ is roughly the same at every position.
We write $\gsens=\|\partial L/\partial\vy_t\|^2$ for the output-side sensitivity at $t$ and $A_c$, $G_c$ for the shares of $\sum_t\|\vx_t\|^2$ and $\sum_t\gsens$ on a class $c$ of positions.
\begin{proposition}[position mismatch]
\label{prop:mismatch}
At the sink-forming down-projection of an instruction-tuned model, $A_{\bos}\ge\AbosMin$ (Llama \LAbos{}, Qwen \QAbos{}) and $G_{\bos}\le\GbosMax$, while $\gsens$ concentrates on the first \Gshare{} of positions, the template tokens, at $10^3$ to $10^5$ times the per-token median (\cref{fig:ag}).
\end{proposition}
The first half is the sink: the residual stream at \bos{} has grown to a norm of several hundred \citep{sun2024massive}, and \cref{eq:layerwise} weights each token by the square of that norm.
The second half follows from the normalisation in front of the next layer.
\begin{lemma}[gradient attenuation at the sink]
\label{lem:rmsnorm}
For $\vh\mapsto\vh/\operatorname{rms}(\vh)$ the Jacobian has operator norm $O(1/\operatorname{rms}(\vh))$, so the gradient reaching the sink position through the normalised path is attenuated by that factor relative to ordinary positions \citep{chen2026gradientsinks}.

\end{lemma}
The output side is large at the template tokens because the response attends to them through the sink, so an error written there is read at every generated token.
The objective thus optimises the one position the loss ignores and is blind to the positions it depends on.
This blind spot is a property of the objective and of the activation statistics, not of a quantiser, which is why AutoRound shares the failure (\cref{sec:phenomenon}).

\subsection{What the compensation does when one token owns the curvature}
\label{sec:mechanism:rankone}
Write $\hess_\lambda=\vx_0\vx_0^\top+\mM$ with $\mM=\sum_{t\ne0}\vx_t\vx_t^\top+\lambda\mI\succ0$ the curvature of the ordinary tokens, $\vv=\mM^{-1}\vx_0$ and $\kappa=\vx_0^\top\vv$.
Under \cref{prop:mismatch}, $\kappa\gg1$ (\cref{app:extra}).
\begin{theorem}[compensation under a dominant token]
\label{thm:rankone}
(a) For every column $j$ and unrounded column $k\ne j$, the update \cref{eq:obs} is exactly
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact}
\Delta\vw_k=\delta_j\,\frac{v_jv_k/(1+\kappa)-[\mM^{-1}]_{jk}}{[\mM^{-1}]_{jj}-v_j^2/(1+\kappa)} .
\end{equation}
(b) If $\mM=\lambda\mI$ this reduces to $\Delta\vw_k=\delta_j\,x_{0j}x_{0k}/(\lambda+\|\vx_0\|^2-x_{0j}^2)$: the update is a multiple of $\vx_0$ restricted to the unrounded columns, and the output change at \bos{} is $-\delta_jx_{0j}\lambda/(\lambda+\|\vx_0\|^2-x_{0j}^2)\to0$.
(c) In the same case the output change at any position $t$, to leading order in $\lambda/\|\vx_0\|^2$ and $x_{0j}^2/\|\vx_0\|^2$, is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:error}
e_t=\delta_j\,x_{0j}\,\frac{\vx_0^\top\vx_t}{\|\vx_0\|^2}\;-\;\delta_j\,x_{tj},
\end{equation}
so that after rounding a set $J$ of columns, row $r$ carries $e^{(r)}_t=c_r\,\vx_0^\top\vx_t/\|\vx_0\|^2+\eta^{(r)}_t$ with $c_r=\sum_{j\in J}\delta^{(r)}_jx_{0j}$ and $\eta^{(r)}_t=-\sum_{j\in J}\delta^{(r)}_jx_{tj}$, up to the interaction between successive steps.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) By Sherman--Morrison, $\hess_\lambda^{-1}=\mM^{-1}-\vv\vv^\top/(1+\kappa)$; substituting $[\hess_\lambda^{-1}]_{jk}=[\mM^{-1}]_{jk}-v_jv_k/(1+\kappa)$ into \cref{eq:obs} gives \cref{eq:exact}, and the denominator is positive because $\hess_\lambda\succ0$.
(b) With $\mM=\lambda\mI$, $\vv=\vx_0/\lambda$ and $\kappa=\|\vx_0\|^2/\lambda$; for $k\ne j$ the off-diagonal $[\mM^{-1}]_{jk}$ vanishes and the ratio simplifies as stated.
The rounding itself changes column $j$ by $-\delta_j$, so the output change at \bos{} is $-\delta_jx_{0j}+\sum_{k\ne j}\Delta\vw_kx_{0k}=-\delta_jx_{0j}\big(1-\tfrac{\|\vx_0\|^2-x_{0j}^2}{\lambda+\|\vx_0\|^2-x_{0j}^2}\big)$.
(c) The same sum at a general position gives $-\delta_jx_{tj}+\delta_jx_{0j}(\vx_0^\top\vx_t-x_{0j}x_{tj})/(\lambda+\|\vx_0\|^2-x_{0j}^2)$, whose leading terms are \cref{eq:error}; summing over $J$ row by row gives the decomposition.

\end{proof}
\cref{eq:exact} separates the update into the ordinary OBS compensation through the other tokens' curvature, $[\mM^{-1}]_{jk}$, and a rank-one term along $\vv=\mM^{-1}\vx_0$, the sink pattern whitened by the ordinary tokens.
The rank-one term dominates exactly on the columns where $\vx_0$ carries its mass, and act-order rounds those columns first.
\cref{eq:error} separates the resulting output error into a deterministic part proportional to the overlap $\vx_0^\top\vx_t$ of a position with the sink pattern, and the plain rounding error $-\delta_jx_{tj}$ that RTN would also produce.
\begin{corollary}[the shape of the lesion]
\label{cor:shape}
(i) The compensated error of the matrix is rank-one along $\vx_0$, largest in the rows $r$ with the largest $c_r$, that is, the largest rounding errors on the channels where $\vx_0$ is large; on those rows the update can exceed the group range.
(ii) The output error at a position is the overlap term plus a noise floor: positions whose activation shares the sink direction are damaged in proportion to the overlap, positions orthogonal to it see only rounding noise.
(iii) The relative size of the deterministic term is governed by $\kappa=\vx_0^\top\mM^{-1}\vx_0$; anything that lowers $\kappa$ shrinks it.
\end{corollary}
(i) is the row-\LsinkRow{} concentration and the clipping of \cref{sec:lesion}; (ii) is the template-versus-response contrast, with the noise floor visible in the flat middle quintiles of overlap; (iii) accounts for every remedy found by trial: damping raises $\lambda$ inside $\mM$, token normalisation divides $\vx_0$ by its norm, chat-formatted calibration adds template activations to $\mM$, long windows add ordinary tokens to $\mM$, and re-rounding removes the update.
None of them restores the output side, which is why each has a failure case (\cref{sec:corrected}).

\subsection{Why the correction must be on the token axis}
\label{sec:mechanism:restore}
\begin{proposition}[token-level weights act, channel-level factors cancel]
\label{prop:tokenlevel}
(a) A curvature $\hess\otimes\mG$ with a diagonal output-side factor $\mG=\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_{d_{\mathrm{out}}})$ leaves the update \cref{eq:obs} unchanged.
(b) With $\hessw=\sum_t\wtok\vx_t\vx_t^\top$, $\wtok=\gsens/\|\vx_t\|^2$, the share of $\operatorname{tr}\hessw$ contributed by \bos{} is $G_{\bos}$; under \cref{prop:mismatch} it is at most \GbosMax{}, and the dominant token of \cref{thm:rankone} no longer exists.
(c) $\hessw$ is the per-token-whitened, diagonal-$\mG_t$ approximation of the curvature in \cref{eq:twosides}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) The objective $\operatorname{tr}[\Delta\mW\hess\Delta\mW^\top\mG]=\sum_r\gamma_r\,\Delta\vw^{(r)}\hess\,\Delta\vw^{(r)\top}$ is a sum over rows with a positive scalar per row; the minimiser of each row's term, and hence the column-wise update, does not depend on $\gamma_r$.
(b) $\operatorname{tr}\hessw=\sum_t\wtok\|\vx_t\|^2=\sum_t\gsens$, and the \bos{} term is $g_0$.
(c) Replace $\mG_t$ in \cref{eq:twosides} by $(\gsens/d_{\mathrm{out}})\mI$ and rescale each $\vx_t$ to unit norm; the resulting quadratic form in $\Delta\mW$ is $\operatorname{tr}[\Delta\mW\hessw\Delta\mW^\top]$ up to the constant.
\todo{collaborator: (c) as a statement about the approximation error; and the remark that a weight on the token axis is the only place in \cref{eq:layerwise} where the output side can enter without changing the column-wise update, which is the boundary with \citet{lee2026kronq}.}
\end{proof}
Two limits of the theory should be stated.
It is local to one matrix and, in (c), first order: it predicts the lesion, its direction and its victims, not why the same lesion is harmless in one background and fatal in another (\cref{sec:lesion}).
And \cref{cor:shape}(ii) holds at the extremes of overlap; the middle sixty percent of positions sit at the noise floor $\eta$.
