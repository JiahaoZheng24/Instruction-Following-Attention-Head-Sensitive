\section{Theory: from objective mismatch to error misallocation}
\label{sec:mechanism}

GPTQ and RTN differ only in how they allocate committed rounding error: RTN leaves it where it occurs, GPTQ moves it onto the free columns to reduce the layer-wise objective. At the sink-forming down-projection, the GPTQ objective's curvature is concentrated at \bos{}, while the loss depends on the chat-template positions. GPTQ therefore suppresses the error at \bos{} by shifting it toward loss-relevant positions: an \emph{objective mismatch} becomes an \emph{error misallocation}. The theory covers the formation of the lesion, not its absorption by the rest of the network.


\paragraph{Setup.}
Consider one linear layer $\vy_t=\mW\vx_t$ with $\mW\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$, where $t=0$ denotes the \bos{} position and $\vx_0$ its effective activation accumulated over the calibration samples.
GPTQ quantizes the input coordinates sequentially using the damped input-side curvature $\hess_\lambda=\hess+\lambda\mI$, $\hess=\sum_t\vx_t\vx_t^\top$, $\lambda=\rho\operatorname{tr}\hess/d_{\mathrm{in}}$.
We index the coordinates in quantization order, so that act-order is a permutation applied once before the loop; at step $j$ the free set is the suffix $F_j=\{j,\dots,d_{\mathrm{in}}\}$, and we write $\vx_{t,j}=\vx_t[F_j]$ and, since damping is applied once to the full curvature, $\hess_{\lambda,j}=\hess_\lambda[F_j,F_j]=\hess[F_j,F_j]+\lambda\mI\succ0$.
Column $j$ is rounded from $\vw_j=\mW_{:,j}$ to $\vq_j$, leaving the residual $\delta_j=\vw_j-\vq_j\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$, and every free column $k>j$ is updated by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:obs}
\Delta\vw_k=-\,\delta_j\,\frac{[\hess_{\lambda,j}^{-1}]_{jk}}{[\hess_{\lambda,j}^{-1}]_{jj}} .
\end{equation}
The step changes no column outside $F_j$, so it perturbs the output at position $t$ by $\Delta\vy_t^{(j)}=\Delta\mW^{(j)}[:,F_j]\,\vx_{t,j}$, and the next step rounds an already-modified column with the free set $F_{j+1}$.
RTN performs the same rounding without \cref{eq:obs}; the update is the whole difference between the two.

\subsection{Objective mismatch: reconstruction protects the wrong positions}
\label{sec:mechanism:mismatch}
The layer-wise reconstruction objective measures how much a perturbation changes the output of the current layer, but not how costly that change is to the rest of the network.
To make the missing output side explicit, let $L$ be the loss of the network on the calibration set.
Expanding $L$ to second order in $\Delta\mW$, and neglecting the first-order term as in OBS and cross-position blocks, gives, up to the factor $\tfrac12$ of the expansion, the position-diagonal quadratic term
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:twosides}
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{down}}(\Delta\mW)
=
\sum_t
(\Delta\mW\vx_t)^\top
\mG_t
(\Delta\mW\vx_t),
\qquad
\mG_t
=
\frac{\partial^2 L}
     {\partial\vy_t\,\partial\vy_t^\top}.
\end{equation}
Standard layer-wise reconstruction instead minimizes \cref{eq:layerwise}, $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{layer}}(\Delta\mW)=\sum_t\|\Delta\mW\vx_t\|^2=\operatorname{tr}[\Delta\mW\hess\Delta\mW^\top]$.
Relative to \cref{eq:twosides}, this objective replaces the output metric $\mG_t$ by the same identity at every position, $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{layer}}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{down}}$ with $\mG_t\equiv\mI$. It therefore retains the input-side geometry $\vx_t\vx_t^\top$ but ignores how strongly an output error at position $t$ affects the loss.
The substitution loses nothing for GPTQ when $\mG_t$ is the same at every position (\cref{prop:tokenlevel}(a)), but can change where the error is placed when $\mG_t$ varies across positions. We therefore compare the positional distributions of input-side curvature and downstream sensitivity through $a_t=\|\vx_t\|^2$ and $g_t=\|\partial L/\partial\vy_t\|^2=\operatorname{tr}[(\partial L/\partial\vy_t)(\partial L/\partial\vy_t)^{\top}]$.
Here $g_t$ is the trace of the empirical-Fisher block of position $t$, a measurable proxy for the scale of $\mG_t$ rather than the block itself; like $\vx_0$, $g_0$ is accumulated over the calibration samples.


\begin{proposition}[Position mismatch]
\label{prop:mismatch}
At the sink-forming down-projection, the \bos{} position carries most of the input-side curvature but almost none of the measured downstream sensitivity: $A_{\bos}=a_0/\sum_t a_t\ge\AbosMin{}$ and $G_{\bos}=g_0/\sum_t g_t\le\GbosMax{}$.
Specifically, $A_{\bos}=\AbosOne{}$ for Llama and $\QAbos{}$ for Qwen. The downstream sensitivity instead concentrates on the early template
positions.
\end{proposition}
The attention sink thus makes the two distributions nearly opposite (\cref{fig:ag} shows both shares for 28 models; \cref{app:extra} confirms by injection and activation patching that the loss depends on the template positions), so the layer-wise objective spends most of its curvature on a position the loss barely depends on.
The mismatch is harmless for RTN, which does not consult the objective, but potentially harmful in GPTQ, which redistributes rounding error to reduce \cref{eq:layerwise}.
\Cref{eq:twosides} motivates the weight and is not a quantitative model of the lesion's cost: on Llama the cross-position terms cancel most of its block-diagonal value (\cref{app:negative}), so the surrogate is validated by intervention and repair (\cref{app:extra,sec:corrected}), not by a curvature fit.

\subsection{Compensation turns objective mismatch into error misallocation}
\label{sec:mechanism:rankone}
To see how GPTQ improves its objective while raising the error at individual positions, split the free-block curvature of step $j$ into its \bos{} part and the rest, and define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vkappa}
\begin{gathered}
\hess_{\lambda,j}=\vx_{0,j}\vx_{0,j}^\top+\mM_j,\qquad
\mM_j=\sum_{t\ne0}\vx_{t,j}\vx_{t,j}^\top+\lambda\mI\succ0,\\
\vv=\mM_j^{-1}\vx_{0,j},\qquad \kappa=\vx_{0,j}^\top\mM_j^{-1}\vx_{0,j},\qquad m_{kl}=[\mM_j^{-1}]_{kl},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with the step index suppressed on $\vv$, $\kappa$ and $m_{kl}$ and all indices running over $F_j$.
$\mM_j$ is the curvature of the ordinary tokens plus damping, and $\kappa$ the dominance of \bos{} relative to it.
Empirically, $\kappa\gg1$ at the sink-forming matrices: before the first column is rounded it is \KappaMed{} at Llama's and exceeds $10^{3}$ in fifteen of the \nSinkStats{} models with a sink (per model in \cref{tab:census_full}). It falls along the loop as the sink coordinates leave the free set (\cref{app:proofs:loop}).
\begin{theorem}[Error redistribution under a dominant token]
\label{thm:rankone}
Fix a step $j$: column $j$ is rounded while the columns $k>j$ remain free.
(a) The inverse curvature is exactly $\hess_{\lambda,j}^{-1}=\mM_j^{-1}-\vv\vv^\top/(1+\kappa)$, and the compensation of column $k>j$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact}
\Delta\vw_k=\delta_j\,\frac{v_jv_k/(1+\kappa)-m_{jk}}{m_{jj}-v_j^2/(1+\kappa)} .
\end{equation}
(b) Including the rounding of column $j$, the output perturbation at position $t$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:outgen}
\Delta\vy_t=-\,\delta_j\,\frac{[\mM_j^{-1}\vx_{t,j}]_j-v_j\,\vv^\top\vx_{t,j}/(1+\kappa)}{m_{jj}-v_j^2/(1+\kappa)},\qquad
\Delta\vy_0=-\,\delta_j\,\frac{v_j}{(1+\kappa)\,m_{jj}-v_j^2}.
\end{equation}
(c) In the isotropic case $\mM_j=\lambda\mI$, with $\|\vx_{0,j+1}\|^2=\|\vx_{0,j}\|^2-x_{0j}^2$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:iso}
\begin{gathered}
\Delta\vw_k=\delta_j\,\frac{x_{0j}x_{0k}}{\lambda+\|\vx_{0,j+1}\|^2},\qquad
\Delta\vy_0=-\,\delta_jx_{0j}\,\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+\|\vx_{0,j+1}\|^2},\\
\Delta\vy_t=-\,\delta_jx_{tj}+\delta_j\,x_{0j}\,\frac{\vx_{0,j+1}^\top\vx_{t,j+1}}{\lambda+\|\vx_{0,j+1}\|^2}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\cref{thm:rankone} gives the central mechanism. In the isotropic case of \cref{eq:iso} the first term of $\Delta\vy_t$, $-\delta_jx_{tj}$, is the rounding error RTN would also leave; the second is what the compensation adds. At \bos{} the compensation suppresses this error by a factor $1/(1+\kappa_{j+1})$, $\kappa_{j+1}=\|\vx_{0,j+1}\|^2/\lambda$: when \bos{} dominates the curvature, GPTQ protects \bos{} by redistributing its rounding error onto the remaining coordinates, which improves the layer-wise objective while raising the error at the positions the loss depends on. Objective mismatch becomes error misallocation.

In \cref{eq:exact} the \bos{}-induced component is rank one along $\vv=\mM_j^{-1}\vx_{0,j}$ and, under act-order, strongest in the early steps, when the sink-carrying coordinates are rounded first; \cref{sec:mechanism:signatures} turns this structure into testable signatures.

\subsection{Signatures of the lesion}
\label{sec:mechanism:signatures}

Under the isotropic assumption $\mM_1=\lambda\mI$,  \cref{thm:rankone} yields three directly testable signatures: where the error is written, which positions read it out, and what sets its amplitude.

\begin{corollary}[Signatures of sink-induced redistribution]
\label{cor:shape}
Assume $\mM_1=\lambda\mI$, so that $\mM_j=\lambda\mI$ at every step, and let $J$ be the leading block of sink-carrying
columns.
By \cref{eq:iso}, step $j$ updates every later column $k>j$ by $\Delta\vw^{(j)}_k=\gamma_j\,x_{0k}\,\delta_j$ with $\gamma_j=x_{0j}/(\lambda+\|\vx_{0,j+1}\|^2)$.

(i) \emph{Direction and row concentration.} 
For $k > \max J$, the accumulated sink-induced update satisfies $\Delta\mW^{\mathrm{sink}}_{r,k}=c_r x_{0k}, c_r=\sum_{j\in J}\gamma_j\delta_j^{(r)}$. Thus each affected row follows the sink pattern $\vx_0$, while the accumulated rounding residuals $c_r$ determine its magnitude across rows.

(ii) \emph{Readout.}
The step $j$ updates the output at position $t$ by
$
\gamma_j\,\big(\vx_{0,j+1}^\top\vx_{t,j+1}\big)\,\delta_j :
$
positions whose remaining activations overlap more with the sink pattern
receive more redistributed error.

(iii) \emph{Amplitude.}
The coefficient of each update factorizes as $\gamma_j\,x_{0k}=\frac{\kappa_{j+1}}{1+\kappa_{j+1}}\cdot\frac{x_{0j}\,x_{0k}}{\|\vx_{0,j+1}\|^2}$.
The first factor is the share of the \bos{} rounding error that the step cancels. It is close to one when \bos{} dominates the remaining curvature and close to zero otherwise.
The second factor is at most $|x_{0j}/x_{0k}|$. It can exceed one when $\vx_0$ is concentrated on few coordinates, as the sink is \citep{sun2024massive}.
Together, the two factors can make the update larger than the residual it compensates and can push weights beyond their original range.
\end{corollary}

For general $\mM_j$, the per-step directions differ: (i) becomes a coherent
component along the sink pattern and (ii) an overlap with
$\vv=\mM_j^{-1}\vx_{0,j}$ in place of $\vx_{0,j}$ (\cref{app:proofs:shape}).

\paragraph{What is measured.}
All three hold at Llama's sink matrix (\cref{fig:lesion} and full values in \cref{app:extra}) and across the census (\cref{sec:universality}): the compensation pattern has cosine \PatCosBos{} with $\vx_0$ in \nPatHit{} of \nPatAll{} sink matrices and \LrowShare{} of the template-position error lies in the super-weight row \citep{yu2024superweight}; that error is \LtplErrGPTQ{} for GPTQ against \LtplErrRTN{} for RTN and 0.4 times RTN's at \bos{} (\cref{fig:teaser}); the largest compensated entry is \LmaxQ{} where the largest original weight is \LmaxW{}.
The partly failed prediction of \cref{sec:intro} is the readout: token by token, the overlap places the template positions on Qwen (recall \IsoRecall{}) but not on Llama (\LIsoRecall{}).


\subsection{Restoring the output side on the token axis}
\label{sec:mechanism:restore}
The repair is to put the output side of \cref{eq:twosides} back where the compensation can see it. A position-independent output-channel factor cannot do this, including K-FAC \citep{martens2015kfac}, whose curvature for a linear layer is the Kronecker product of $\hess$ with an output factor averaged over positions.
\begin{proposition}[Token-level weights act, channel-level factors do not]
\label{prop:tokenlevel}
(a) Let $\mG\succeq0$ be independent of position.
Replacing \cref{eq:layerwise} by $\operatorname{tr}[\mG\,\Delta\mW\hess\Delta\mW^\top]$ leaves the column-wise compensation \cref{eq:obs} unchanged; if $\mG$ is diagonal with positive entries, it leaves the nearest-grid rounding within each row unchanged as well.
(b) Let $\hessw=\sum_t\wtok\vx_t\vx_t^\top$ with $\wtok=\gsens/\|\vx_t\|^2$.
Then $\operatorname{tr}\hessw=\sum_t\gsens$, so the \bos{} share of the weighted trace is $G_{\bos}\le\GbosMax{}$ rather than $A_{\bos}$, and the trace no longer singles out \bos{}.
With $\wtok=\gsens$ alone the share is $g_0a_0/\sum_t\gsens a_t$, which is \AGbosL{} for Llama and \AGbosQ{} for Qwen: the dominant token survives.
\end{proposition}
The weighted objective is $\operatorname{tr}[\Delta\mW\hessw\Delta\mW^\top]=\sum_t\gsens\,\|\Delta\mW\vx_t/\|\vx_t\|\|^2$, that is \cref{eq:twosides} with each $\mG_t$ replaced by $\wtok\mI$: the normalization removes the activation-energy dominance that (b) shows $\gsens$ alone cannot remove, and $\gsens$ restores the measured downstream sensitivity (\cref{app:proofs:tokenlevel}); $\hessw$ is a sensitivity-aligned surrogate of the downstream Hessian, not that Hessian.
\cref{sec:corrected} tests it, and (b) predicts the outcome of its ablation.
