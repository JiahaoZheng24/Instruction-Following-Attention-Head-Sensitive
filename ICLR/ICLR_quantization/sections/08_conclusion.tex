\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The layer-wise reconstruction objective is not a neutral proxy for the loss.
In models with an attention sink it is owned, at one layer, by a token whose output the loss ignores, and the compensation step converts that blind spot into an error written along the sink pattern onto the format tokens the loss depends on.
The error is a matrix that can be excised and transplanted, its rows and amplitude follow from \cref{thm:rankone}, the remedies found by trial are the levers of \cref{cor:shape}(iii), and restoring the output side on the token axis (\cref{prop:tokenlevel}) repairs every collapse we found.
In practice a quantized instruction-tuned model should be validated on free generation and, if an early down-projection is sink-dominated, calibrated with \cref{eq:corrected} or chat-formatted text.

\paragraph{Limitations and open questions.}
The theory is local to one matrix and exact for the steps that write the lesion; the effect of the columns rounded afterwards is measured, not derived, and it predicts the lesion, not why one quantized network absorbs it and another does not.
Why that absorption succeeds for one calibration draw and fails for another is not derived, there is no cross-model criterion, and Mistral's window collapse is localized to two layers but not to a matrix.
The corrected objective lowers Llama's MMLU by a point, recovers GSM8K only partly and costs several points on a model without a sink; quantization is simulated and the census stops at 32B.
Thirty-one pre-registered predictions failed (\cref{app:negative}), among them a $\kappa$ criterion and the collapse of GPTQModel's own default loop, whose group-aware column order the theory does not cover.
