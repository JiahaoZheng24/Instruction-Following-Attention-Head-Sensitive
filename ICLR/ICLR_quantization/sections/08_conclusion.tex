\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The layer-wise reconstruction objective is not a neutral proxy for the loss.
In models with an attention sink it is owned, at one layer, by a token whose output the loss ignores, and the compensation step converts that blind spot into a one-directional error on the tokens the loss depends on.
The error is a matrix that can be excised and transplanted; its direction and its victims follow from \cref{thm:rankone}; the remedies practitioners found by trial are instances of \cref{cor:shape}(iii); and restoring the output side, by weighting each calibration token with its output-side sensitivity over its input energy, repairs every collapse we found.
In practice, a quantised instruction-tuned model should be validated on free generation, and if an early down-projection is sink-dominated, which one forward pass reveals, it should be calibrated with \cref{eq:corrected} or with chat-formatted text and compared against RTN, at 4 bits as well as at 3.

\paragraph{Limitations and open questions.}
Natural collapses are rare, and the condition that makes a lesion fatal is demonstrated by transplants, not derived: the lesion is benign in a full-precision or RTN network and fatal inside a GPTQ solution, its own or a foreign one, so fatality belongs to the joint solution of the lesion and the layers quantised after it, and deriving when those layers amplify the error rather than absorb it is the open problem this paper leaves.
What we can exclude is a fragility of the full-precision network, which tolerates a synthetic perturbation along $\sinkdir$ at four times the lesion's amplitude, and an accumulation over the response, whose tokens are barely affected.
The corrected objective lowers Llama's MMLU by a point relative to the collapsed checkpoint and recovers GSM8K only partly, and on a model without a sink it costs several points.
Quantisation is simulated; the quantisers covered are those with an input-side curvature or a token-averaged block loss, not lattice methods; the census stops at 32B.
About thirty pre-registered predictions failed (\cref{app:negative}), among them that collapse could be predicted from the lesion's size, induced by injecting its direction, or confined to a few layers; the account that remains is the one that survived them.
