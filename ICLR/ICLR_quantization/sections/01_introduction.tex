\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Weight-only post-training quantization is how most instruction-tuned language models are deployed, and GPTQ \citep{frantar2023gptq} is its default engine: each linear layer is rounded column by column, and every rounding error is compensated on the remaining columns using the curvature of the layer's reconstruction error on a small calibration set.
The procedure is validated by perplexity and multiple-choice accuracy, and \cref{fig:bars} shows what these metrics do not see.
Quantized to 3 bits by reference GPTQ on the short-document calibration that widely used libraries sample by default, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct keeps an MMLU score above its round-to-nearest (RTN) counterpart and a perplexity of the same order, and stops following instructions: IFEval falls from \LfpIF{} to \LgptqIF{}, while RTN, which uses no calibration data, retains \LrtnIF{}; Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct behaves the same way, falling from \QfpIF{} to \QgptqIF{} against \QrtnIF{} for RTN.

Three properties explain why this has not been reported: the failure is silent, because the metrics never present the tokens that carry the damage; it is rare, two of the \nCensus{} instruction-tuned models we tested under the default protocol; and it depends on how the calibration text is cut, the same model, code and sample count with 2,048-token windows in place of short documents giving \LcwinIF{}.
The layer-wise objective weights each calibration token by its squared activation and treats an output error as equally harmful at every position (\cref{fig:teaser}).
At the layer that forms the attention sink both assumptions fail at once: the beginning-of-sequence token owns the curvature, and the loss is indifferent to that token's output while depending on the format tokens that attend to it.
Under a curvature owned by one token each compensation step has an exact closed form, and it turns this blind spot into what we call the lesion: an error written into one matrix along the sink pattern, read out by the format tokens.
The lesion can be excised without extra bits and transplanted between networks; it is necessary for the collapse but not sufficient: on Qwen it collapses a round-to-nearest network alone, on Llama it needs a second, weaker copy of itself one layer earlier, which only the short-document protocol turns into a sink, and a quantized network survives it only when the matrices quantized after it absorb it.
The direct remedy, weighting each token by its output-side sensitivity over its input energy, repairs every collapse we found.

Our contributions are a silent, protocol-dependent failure of layer-wise quantization and its localization to one or two matrices (\cref{sec:phenomenon,sec:lesion}); a derivation of the lesion from the objective, exact under a dominant token, with its rows and amplitude as confirmed predictions, its direction as one it shares with the ordinary columns and its readout as a partly failed one, whose premise holds in all \nSink{} models with an attention sink and in neither model without (\cref{sec:mechanism,sec:universality}); and the proof that a position-independent output factor cannot change the column-wise update while a token weight can, with the corrected objective this gives and its costs (\cref{sec:corrected}).
The theory covers the formation of the lesion, not its absorption by the rest of the network; every experiment after the diagnosis was pre-registered (failed predictions: \cref{app:negative}).
