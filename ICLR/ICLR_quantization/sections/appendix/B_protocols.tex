\section{Protocols and implementation details}
\label{app:protocols}

\subsection{Calibration sampling in the reference implementations}
We verified the sampling of each protocol against the source of the implementation that uses it (September 2026).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{GPTQ reference code} \citep{frantar2023gptq}: draws documents of at least 2,048 tokens and cuts one random 2,048-token window from each; our c4win and Pile protocols follow this.
\item \textbf{GPTQModel} \citep{gptqmodel}, \texttt{utils/calibration.py}: tokenizes each calibration row separately, does not truncate below the model's maximum length and does not concatenate rows by default; the README example takes 1,024 rows of C4. Its \texttt{QuantizeConfig} defaults are symmetric quantization, group size 128, \texttt{damp\_percent} 0.05, \texttt{true\_sequential} on and \texttt{desc\_act} unset. Our short-document protocol reproduces its sampling with 128 rows; with 1,024 rows and the same damping Llama gives \LgptqLibIF{} and Qwen \QgptqLibIF{}.
\item \textbf{LLM Compressor} \citep{llmcompressor}, \texttt{transformers/data/base.py}: truncates each sample to \texttt{max\_seq\_length} and does not concatenate by default, the same sampling shape; its documented example for instruction-tuned models uses chat-formatted data, which is one of the remedies of \cref{cor:shape}.
\end{itemize}
Our quantizer matches the reference loop of GPTQ with act-order and $\rho=0.05$ and collects the Hessians of the seven matrices of a block from the block's unquantized input (no true-sequential pass); RTN uses the same grouping and symmetric grid.

\paragraph{The library's own loop.}
A pre-registered prediction that GPTQModel's end-to-end default path collapses both models failed (\cref{tab:library}).
With nothing but bits and group size set, gptqmodel 5.6.12 on the README recipe (the first 1,024 or 128 rows of one C4 shard, tokenized by the library one row at a time, \bos{} added) leaves Llama at \LibLoneKIF{} / \LibLIF{} and Qwen at \LibQoneKIF{} / \LibQIF{}, above RTN.
Changing one setting at a time on Llama with 128 rows places the difference in the column order: act-order (\texttt{desc\_act}) collapses (\LibLdescIF{}), the natural order with the library's group-aware reordering switched off collapses harder (\LibLnogarIF{}), and our loop's three settings together collapse (\LibLourcfgIF{}), whereas collecting the Hessians without the true-sequential pass (\LibLnotseqIF{}), fixing the damping (\LibLfixdampIF{}), feeding the paper's document stream (\LibLpapercalIF{}) or pre-tokenized rows (\LibLpretokIF{}) change nothing.
GPTQModel's current default, group-aware reordering, sorts the columns inside each 128-column group by $\operatorname{diag}\hess$ and ranks the groups, keeping every group contiguous; the reference code's act-order sorts all columns globally, and the natural order sorts none.
The same holds inside our loop: with the group-aware order in place of act-order and nothing else changed, Llama scores \GarLIF{} and Qwen \GarQIF{} (\cref{tab:library}), and the natural order collapses Llama (\NoaoLIF{}).
The mechanism statistics of the sink matrix (\cref{sec:mechanism:signatures}) show what the order changes. Under act-order the compensation displaces the weights of Llama's sink matrix by 32\% of their norm, protects \bos{} (output error 0.4 times RTN's) and raises the error at every template position 2 to 10 times and at the user text 3 times; under the group-aware order the displacement is 0.3\%, \bos{} is protected a hundredfold (0.01 times RTN's) and the error rises at one position only, the token that closes the header (6.6 times), the others staying within 1.2 times RTN's; under the natural order, which leaves the \bos{} coordinates free for the first 2,400 steps, the same position reaches 41 times RTN's. Qwen behaves alike (displacement 17\% against 2.3\%, the worst template position 22 against 5.6 times).
The lesion signature of \cref{sec:universality} (squared error at the worst template position at least 3 times RTN's) is therefore present under every order, 105 / 43 / 1,700 on Llama and 471 / 32 on Qwen for act-order / group-aware / natural, and what the order sets is how much of the sink's rounding error is redistributed and over how many positions; why the group-wise order confines it is not derived (\cref{sec:conclusion}).
The collapse of this paper is that of the reference loop and of the natural order under the libraries' sampling shape, not of GPTQModel's current default; LLM Compressor was not run end to end.

\subsection{A correction to our layer-wise loop}
An earlier version of our loop passed the keyword arguments captured from the model's forward call, including an automatically created key-value cache, into the per-layer calls.
Because each layer is called twice per sample (once to accumulate the Hessian, once to propagate after quantization), the second call attended the layer's pre-quantization keys and values.
Every number in this paper comes from the corrected loop, which we validated by comparing the propagated activations at every depth with a full forward pass of the quantized model (relative error $0$ at all layers for Llama and Qwen), and every GPTQ-family arm was re-run under it.
The two collapses, their cures and the census statements were unchanged; two results moved.
Llama under Pile windows went from a collapsed value (0.402) to a healthy one (\LpileIF{}), so the protocol dependence for Llama is now carried by the short-document protocol alone.
And the transplant of the short-document lesion into the window-calibrated network went from a mild loss (0.567) to a collapse (\TPcwin{}): under the defective loop the later layers had been compensated against partially unquantized activations and were less tightly fitted to their own sink-forming matrix, which is why a foreign matrix was tolerated.
We report the corrected values and draw the conclusion of \cref{sec:lesion} from them.

\subsection{The corrected objective in practice}
\label{app:corrected}
$\gsens=\|\partial L/\partial\vy_t\|^2$ is measured on the full-precision model before quantization: one forward and one backward pass per calibration sample with $L$ the summed next-token cross-entropy of that sample, hooks on the input and on the output gradient of every linear layer, parameters frozen (activation gradients only).
The weights $\wtok=\gsens/\|\vx_t\|^2$ are normalized to unit mean over the calibration set per layer and are used without a cap or a floor; the capped variant of \cref{tab:ablation_full} is the only exception.
Because the damping is $\lambda=\rho\operatorname{mean}\operatorname{diag}\hessw$, it follows the reweighted curvature: at the sink-forming layer $\operatorname{tr}\hessw$ no longer contains the \bos{} mass, so $\lambda$ falls by the same factor and stays at $\rho$ times the ordinary-token curvature; $\rho$ was not retuned (\cref{tab:seeds} lists the sensitivity to $\rho$: \CorrRhoLow{} at $\rho=0.01$, \CorrRhoHigh{} at $\rho=0.2$ against \LcorrIF{} at $0.05$, Llama).
The sensitivity is that of the full-precision network; whether it should be re-measured on the partially quantized network as the layer-wise loop proceeds is left open.
Cost on one NVIDIA H200 GPU, 128 short documents, quantization only: on Llama-3.1-8B GPTQ takes \CostGptq{} and the corrected objective \CostCorr{}, with peak memory \MemGptq{} against \MemCorr{}; on Qwen2.5-32B \CostQthirtytwoGptq{} against \CostQthirtytwo{} and \MemQthirtytwoGptq{} against \MemQthirtytwo{}.
The extra cost is one backward pass per calibration sample and the storage of $\gsens$, which grows with the layer width while the weights grow with its square, so the relative memory overhead falls with model size (49\% at 8B, 13\% at 32B).
On Qwen2.5-14B the corrected objective takes \CostQfourteen{} and \MemQfourteen{} against \CostQfourteenGptq{} and \MemQfourteenGptq{} for GPTQ, but the two were measured in different submissions and possibly on different nodes, so only the 8B and 32B pairs are within-batch comparisons.
The gradient is not needed at every layer.
\Cref{prop:mismatch} fails, and the lesion forms, only where a down-projection is sink-dominated, which the forward pass that builds $\hess$ already reveals through $A_{\bos}$; hooking only those layers changes the storage but not the time, since the backward pass is the same whether one layer or all are read from it, and re-rounding those layers without compensation (\cref{sec:lesion}) needs no gradient at all.
The position prior of \cref{sec:corrected}, a constant weight on the first positions of every sample, is the gradient-free surrogate: it repairs every collapse we have at no cost; whether it holds on a model whose $\gsens$ sits away from the first positions is untested.

\subsection{Evaluation}
Every generation benchmark uses the model's own chat template and greedy decoding in bf16.
IFEval is the 541-prompt set of \citet{zhou2023ifeval} with the official checker, at most 1,280 new tokens, reported as the mean of prompt-level strict, instruction-level strict, prompt-level loose and instruction-level loose accuracy; \cref{tab:collapse} and the census report this mean.
Multi-IF is the English subset of \citet{he2024multiif}, three turns, each turn appended to the running conversation and scored with the IFEval instruction registry against that turn's response; we report prompt-level strict accuracy per turn.
GSM8K is the 1,319-problem test set, zero-shot with a chain-of-thought instruction in the chat template, at most 512 new tokens, the answer being the number after \texttt{\#\#\#\#} or, failing that, the last number in the response.
MMLU is five-shot with the standard harness prompt, scored by comparing the logits of the four answer letters.
Perplexity is on the WikiText-2 raw test set, non-overlapping 2,048-token windows, token-weighted mean negative log-likelihood.
On Llama and Mistral the chat template writes a \bos{} token and the tokenizer adds another, so every generation prompt in this paper carries two; all arms share this, and the single-\bos{} control moves the full-precision Llama from \LfpIF{} to \LfpOneBosIF{} and the collapsed one from \LgptqIF{} to \LgptqOneBosIF{}, so no conclusion depends on it. The position indices of \cref{sec:lesion} count both.
One arm is one checkpoint; IFEval takes about twenty minutes per 8B checkpoint on one H200 and the quantization itself the times of \cref{app:corrected}.
The \SeedNoise{}-point noise threshold of \cref{sec:phenomenon} is the largest movement between two calibration draws of any non-collapsing arm we ran, rounded up: the corrected objective on Llama moves from \LcorrIF{} to \LcorrSeedIF{} (\cref{tab:seeds}), the other pairs by less.
