\section{Generality}
\label{sec:universality}
The parts of the account generalise to different degrees, and we state each with the number of models behind it; every statement was written as a prediction before the models were run.
The premise, \cref{prop:mismatch}, holds in every one of \nSink{} instruction-tuned models with a sink-dominated down-projection ($A_{\bos}\ge\AbosMin$, $G_{\bos}\le\GbosMax$) and in neither gemma-2 model, whose architecture has no such layer (largest $A_{\bos}$ \GemmaAbosMax{}); the boundary is the sink, not the model family.
The lesion follows wherever the premise holds and the quantiser is OBS-based: at the sink-forming down-projection of each of the \nSinkStats{} census models with a sink, the error of GPTQ on the template positions exceeds RTN's by a factor between \LesionRatioMin{} (Qwen2.5-7B) and \LesionRatioMax{} (Qwen2.5-14B), in \nRowConc{} of them the share of the error carried by the largest row is more than twice what RTN leaves there, and in \nClipOver{} the largest compensated entry exceeds twice the largest original entry, as \cref{cor:shape}(i) predicts (\cref{app:extra}).
Collapse does not follow: \nCollapse{} of \nCensus{} models collapse under short documents and one of \nWin{} under c4win, and models whose lesion is as large as Nemo's or Qwen2.5-32B's do not; the size of the lesion sets no threshold.
Collapse is rare because it needs the lesion and the background together.
The corrected objective is bounded below: on the \nCorr{} census models with an RTN reference it never falls more than \CorrWorstVsRTN{} points below RTN, and the one case beyond noise is gemma-2-9b, which has no sink and therefore nothing for the correction to repair; it repairs every 3-bit collapse we found and the two largest 4-bit losses, and on the \nHealthy{} models that were not collapsing it changes IFEval by \CorrHealthyMean{} on average (\CorrHealthyWorst{} to \CorrHealthyBest{}).
\cref{tab:census} in the appendix lists the counts.
