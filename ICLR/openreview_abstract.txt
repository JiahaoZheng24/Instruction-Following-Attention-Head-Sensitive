Quantized instruction-tuned language models are usually validated with perplexity and multiple-choice accuracy; these metrics can miss a collapse of instruction following. At 3 bits, reference GPTQ with act-order under the short-document calibration sampling that widely used libraries apply by default lowers the IFEval score of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct from 0.768 to 0.155 and that of Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct from 0.820 to 0.426, below round-to-nearest at 0.565 and 0.697, while MMLU stays in the ordinary 3-bit range. The failure is rare, two of eighteen models, and depends on how the calibration text is cut and, for Llama, on which documents are drawn. We trace it to the MLP down-projection of the layer that forms the attention sink: its calibration Hessian is effectively rank-one and owned by the beginning-of-sequence token, whereas the measured loss sensitivity is near zero at that token and concentrated on the prompt-template tokens, so the layer-wise objective, which sees only the input side, optimizes for the one token that does not matter. Under a dominant token each compensation step of GPTQ has an exact closed form, which predicts the matrix, rows and amplitude of the damage. Excision and transplantation show that the damaged matrix is necessary but not sufficient: on Qwen it alone collapses a round-to-nearest network, on Llama it needs a second, weaker copy of itself one layer earlier, which only the short-document protocol creates, and a quantized network survives it only when the matrices quantized after it absorb it. Weighting each calibration token by its measured output-side sensitivity divided by its input energy removes every 3-bit collapse we found and, across eighteen models, never falls more than 4.6 points below round-to-nearest. This mismatch is present in all twenty-six models with an attention sink and absent in the two without.
