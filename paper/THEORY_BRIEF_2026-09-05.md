# Theory note: GPTQ collapse as calibration-set overfitting

Scope. Two discrete phenomena need a mathematical account: (i) on the same model, GPTQ can lose to round-to-nearest (RTN) by 30–40 IFEval points while winning its own layer objective by 2.5×; (ii) three unrelated interventions (Hessian dampening, in-distribution calibration, exempting the most salient coordinates) cure the collapse and reach the same score. The continuous bits-vs-loss laws (2411.04330, 2411.17691) do not produce either phenomenon and are out of scope. All quantities below are logged in `runs/stats/*/stats.csv`; every proposition is paired with the data that tests it.

---

## 1. Setup and notation

One linear layer with weight $W\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ (rows = output units, columns = input channels). Everything below is per row; $w\in\mathbb{R}^n$ is one row.

- **Calibration inputs** $X_c\in\mathbb{R}^{N_c\times n}$ (128 c4 documents), $H_c = \tfrac{2}{N_c}X_c^\top X_c$.
- **Deployment inputs** $X_d$ (chat-formatted prompts), $H_d = \tfrac{2}{N_d}X_d^\top X_d$. Both are logged (`obj_*` uses $H_c$, `obj_*_alt` uses $H_d$).
- **Grid.** 3-bit symmetric, groups of 128 columns, per-row scale $s_g = 2\max_{j\in g}|w_j|/7$; $\mathrm{rnd}_g(x) = s_g(\mathrm{clip}(\mathrm{round}(x/s_g)+4,0,7)-4)$.
- **RTN**: $q^{\mathrm{rtn}}_j = \mathrm{rnd}(w_j)$ independently per coordinate.
- **Layer objectives** for a quantized row $q$: $E_c(q) = (w-q)^\top H_c (w-q)$ (what GPTQ minimises) and $E_d(q) = (w-q)^\top H_d (w-q)$ (what deployment sees).
- **GPTQ (OBS, column-sequential).** Dampen $H_\lambda = H_c + \lambda I$, $\lambda = \rho\cdot\mathrm{mean}(\mathrm{diag}H_c)$ ($\rho$ = `percdamp`; frozen protocol 0.05, GPTQ reference 0.01, GPTQModel default 0.1). Let $U$ be the upper Cholesky factor of $H_\lambda^{-1}$. Columns are processed in act-order (descending $\mathrm{diag}H_c$). At column $j$:
  $$q_j = \mathrm{rnd}(w_j),\qquad e_j = \frac{w_j-q_j}{U_{jj}},\qquad w_{k} \leftarrow w_k - e_j U_{jk}\quad (k>j).$$
  In block form (`gptq_core.py`): rounding errors of earlier columns are regressed onto later columns using $H_\lambda$.
- **Protection (TaCQ / hmag)**: a set $P$ of coordinates is never rounded; they stay free (fp16), absorb the OBS updates of earlier columns, and inject zero error.
- **Dampening limit** (verified numerically): $\rho\to\infty \Rightarrow$ GPTQ $\to$ RTN.

Across 17 models the module-median $E_d$ ratio ranks the two collapsing models first and second (1.02, 0.91; all others ≤ 0.86). Clipping fraction is 0.2 % in every model (clipping is not the mechanism). Condition numbers of $H_\lambda$ do not separate the models.

Summary: GPTQ always wins its own objective by ~2.5×, but on the two collapsing models it does **not** win the deployment objective; ridge regularisation, in-distribution calibration, or exempting the most salient coordinates all restore the win.

## 3. Tractable relaxation

Exact analysis of the sequential rounding is a lattice problem (GPTQ is Babai's nearest-plane algorithm on the lattice generated by the Cholesky factor of $H_c$; 2507.18553, Thm 5, bounds $E_c$ under a no-clipping assumption). The following relaxation keeps the essential structure.

**Two-block model.** Split coordinates into a rounded set $S$ and a free set $F$ (the coordinates still receiving compensation; under protection $P\subseteq F$ permanently). Let $\delta = q_S - w_S$ be the rounding error on $S$ (RTN-style, bounded coordinate-wise by $s_g/2$). OBS/GPTQ sets the free block to the least-squares optimum under the calibration Hessian:
$$w_F^\star = w_F + A_\lambda\,\delta,\qquad A_\lambda = -(H_{c,FF}+\lambda I)^{-1}H_{c,FS}.$$
Compensation is therefore a **ridge regression of the rounding error onto the free coordinates using calibration covariance**; RTN is $A=0$.

Deployment error of the compensated row relative to RTN with the same $\delta$:
$$\Delta_d \;=\; E_d(\text{GPTQ})-E_d(\text{RTN}) \;=\; \delta^\top\!\left(A_\lambda^\top H_{d,FF}A_\lambda + 2\,H_{d,SF}A_\lambda\right)\!\delta .$$
With $H_d = H_c$ and $\lambda=0$ this equals $-\,\delta^\top H_{c,SF}H_{c,FF}^{-1}H_{c,FS}\,\delta \le 0$: compensation never hurts in-distribution. Under shift it can be positive.

## 4. Propositions (ordered by value to the paper)

**P1 (in-distribution optimality).** In the two-block model with $H_d=H_c$: $\Delta_d\le 0$ for all $\lambda\ge 0$, and $\Delta_d$ is monotone non-decreasing in $\lambda$.

**P2 (shift bound: when compensation hurts).** With $\Delta H = H_d - H_c$ (blocks $\Delta H_{FF},\Delta H_{SF}$), a bound of the form
$$\Delta_d \;\le\; -\,\delta^\top G_\lambda\delta \;+\; \|\delta\|^2\big(\|A_\lambda\|^2\|\Delta H_{FF}\| + 2\|A_\lambda\|\,\|\Delta H_{SF}\|\big),$$
where $G_\lambda \succeq 0$ is the calibration gain (e.g. $H_{c,SF}(H_{c,FF}+\lambda I)^{-1}(2\lambda I + H_{c,FF})(H_{c,FF}+\lambda I)^{-1}H_{c,FS}$). Compensation hurts iff the shift term exceeds the calibration gain. Since $\|A_\lambda\|\le \|H_{c,FS}\|/(\lambda_{\min}(H_{c,FF})+\lambda)$, dampening shrinks the shift term quadratically and the gain only linearly, so there is a finite $\lambda$ above which $\Delta_d<0$ again. Every quantity on the right is measured: $\Delta H$ from the two Hessians, $\|A_\lambda\|$ implicitly via `send_mass`. The cleanest form of $G_\lambda$ and the tightest norm choice are open.

**P3 (ridge path and the sharp threshold).** $\Delta_d(\lambda)$ is a rational function of $\lambda$. Characterise $\lambda^\star=\arg\min_\lambda E_d$ and show $\lambda^\star>0$ iff $\Delta H\ne 0$ on the relevant subspace. Open: the empirical curve is a **step** (Llama .152 at $\rho$=1, .578 at $\rho$=2), not a smooth optimum. Candidate explanation: the network loss is a threshold function of layer error (once the early-layer residual-stream cosine falls below ~0.5 generation enters a repetition attractor); layer-level $E_d(\lambda)$ may be smooth. Per-layer $E_d$ exists only at $\rho\in\{0.05, 5\}$; more points can be logged.

**P4 (protection = optimal sparse removal of over-fitted coordinates).** Exempting coordinate $j\in S$ sets $\delta_j=0$. In the two-block model the best $k$ coordinates to exempt maximise the reduction of $\delta^\top M\delta$, $M = A^\top H_{d,FF}A + 2H_{d,SF}A$. Under a diagonal-dominance assumption this ranks coordinates by $|\delta_j|\cdot\|M_{\cdot j}\|$, i.e. rounding error × activation scale, which is why the gradient-free criterion $|w_j|\sqrt{H_{jj}}$ and TaCQ's $|w_j|\,|\nabla_j L|\,|\delta_j|$ rescue equally (.629–.657 vs .643–.671 on Llama; .750 vs .734 on Q14) and why *post-hoc* restoration of the same coordinates does nothing (the damage is $A\delta$ on $F$, not on $S$). This is the "protection ≈ regularisation" statement.

**P5 (why RTN cannot collapse this way).** RTN error is coordinate-wise bounded and $H$-independent: $E_d(\text{RTN}) \le \tfrac{1}{4}\sum_j s_{g(j)}^2 (H_d)_{jj}$. GPTQ's displacement on $F$ is $A_\lambda\delta$, unbounded in the ill-conditioned directions of $H_{c,FF}$ as $\lambda\to 0$ (the Cholesky failure at $\rho=0.001$ is the extreme case). The Babai bound (2507.18553) controls $E_c$ via the LDL pivots of $H_c$; the deployment analogue needs $H_d$.

**P6 (sequential exactness, optional).** The two-block relaxation is exact for the last column; the real algorithm nests it $n$ times. Show that the sequential scheme equals the block formula applied to the *current* (already-compensated) $w_F$, so the propositions hold step-wise with $\delta$ replaced by the running error (`Err1 @ Hinv[i1:i2, i2:]` in `gptq_core.py`).

## 5. Target for the paper's theory section

Minimum: P1 and P2 stated with short proofs, plus one figure of measured $\Delta_d$ per module (from `stats.csv`) against the bound's two terms for three models, showing that the collapsing models are those where the shift term wins. P4 as the proposition that explains the equivalence of the three cures. P3 and P5 as remarks. No bits-vs-loss scaling.
